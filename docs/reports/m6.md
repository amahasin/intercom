## Load test (100 people on host relay)

Two full cores act as hosts:

- **Host 1:** pinned to logical cores 0–7, the plan's minimum relay host, which is also how its CPU is reported.
- **Host 2:** pinned to cores 8–15; it takes over when host 1 is killed.

98 simulated participants run in four processes on the remaining cores. Each has its own identity, TLS links and the real call manager; its media is mediasoup with plain RTP over SRTP to the host. 20 participants have a microphone (three loud at a time, changing every 4 s) and 16 have a camera (three simulcast layers, 150/500/1,200 kbps). Everyone asks for 360p.

Host CPU here is far below the 70% limit. The plan's 400 Mbps budget is about the same as the measured traffic.

## What was built

- **Host relay:**
  - `packages/core/src/relay`: mediasoup in the core process, one worker per core (up to 16, started as the meeting grows), producers piped between routers.
  - What each person receives: the loudest 3 voices, up to 9 cameras (the most recent speakers, paged), and the screen share. Unwatched cameras pause at their sender.
  - The roster lives at the host; reactions and organiser controls go through it.
  - Joiners above 25 people come in muted with their camera off.
- **Relay in the call manager:**
  - The mesh-to-relay upgrade, the host choice (the organiser if capable, else the strongest), and the 25-person cap when no PC is capable.
  - Hand-over on link loss, 6 s of heartbeat silence or the host leaving, with a guard against splitting the meeting.
  - A meeting that cannot take a joiner tells them at once.
- **Relay in the renderer and on Windows:**
  - Renderer: mediasoup-client with 3-layer simulcast, a "Reconnecting" banner, the host's load line, camera paging, and "+N" tiles in large meetings.
  - Capability: wired gigabit and 8 cores, measured with `Get-NetAdapter` and no elevation.
  - Firewall rules for the relay worker on UDP and TCP 47802–47817.
- **Sealed envelopes:** ECDH P-256, HKDF-SHA256, AES-256-GCM with a one-time key. Up to 3 carriers, a 50 MB store (10 MB per depositor), 7 days. Carriers hand over on beacon or link; recipients also fetch on link-up. The sender's status reads "Will be delivered when … comes online".
- **Other subnets:** "Add colleague by address" (TOFU dial), `peers.exchange` lists, beacons over the link every 15 s, re-dial every minute, and a remote-peer count in the network status.
- **Moving PCs:** a passphrase-protected export (PBKDF2-SHA256 at 600,000 iterations, then AES-256-GCM) of the key, the database snapshot and the avatar. Import checks and stages the profile, which swaps in at the next start; the old profile is kept.
- **Updates:** a daily check of a shared folder, with the Authenticode publisher check done by the main process. A silent install relaunches the app. The banner reads "A colleague is running a newer version".
- **Signing:** electron-builder signs everything (app, relay worker, installer, uninstaller) when `CSC_LINK` is set. This was exercised with a throwaway self-signed certificate; the release installers are unsigned (no office certificate).
- **Diagnostics:** a rotating log file in the profile, a Diagnostics section in Settings, and a "Save diagnostics file" export. An About section lists the known limits.
- **Packaging:** mediasoup's JavaScript is bundled (with a build plugin keeping only its version from `package.json`, so no URLs enter the app). The worker ships as `resources/relay/mediasoup-worker.exe` (x64; ARM64 builds carry it but never start it). The smoke test checks it is there.
- **Test tools:** the relay load test (`tools/sim/src/relay-load`), simulated relay media (`relay-media.ts`), scripted actions and restarts for process peers, subnets in the in-process simulator, and the soak harness (`tests/e2e/soak.ts`).

## Bugs found and fixed during M6

- **Split meetings after a join burst:**
  - A joiner received a consumer for every stream at once, as separate frames. That broke the 50-frames-per-second link limit; the participant banned the host's link and started a needless hand-over.
  - Fixed: notifications are gathered for 50 ms into one frame, consumer resumes are batched, and a would-be successor refuses to take over while it still hears the host.
- **Roster snapshots raced with join notices:** a full roster that arrived after a "someone joined" notice marked that person as left. Snapshots now only add and update.
- **A link blip wiped a participant's media:** a re-join replaced the member at the host, closing their transports. A re-join now keeps the media connection.
- **Hosts not yet heard:** a participant whose process had not yet heard a host's beacon declared it dead at once. Connects now retry for up to 3 s.
- **mediasoup's install-time helpers (`node-fetch`, `tar` and their dependencies) were packaged into the app.** mediasoup's JavaScript is now bundled and only the worker executable ships.
- **Test tooling:**
  - The load harness leaked simulated receive streams, slowing its own processes over time.
  - It timed recovery by polling, which overstated it; it now uses the participants' own clocks.
  - A failure inside the M4 stall sampler hid the real error.

## Known limitations

- **Relay privacy:** the relay host decrypts and forwards the meeting's media (it is a participant). The About section says so. End-to-end media encryption through the relay is not in the plan.
- **ARM64 PCs never host:** mediasoup has no Windows ARM64 worker, so ARM64 PCs join relayed meetings but never host them.
- **Envelopes reveal the recipient:** carriers learn that an envelope exists for a recipient, and its size; they learn neither the content nor the sender.
- **Two copies of one profile:** a profile imported on a new PC and still used on the old one makes two PCs with one identity; the Settings text says to uninstall on the old PC.
- **Not tested on this PC:** the standard-user install and update under a separate Windows account, and real SmartScreen behaviour (M6.5).
- **Old peers and large meetings:** relayed meetings need every participant on 0.6.0, since an M5 peer lacks `calls.relay`. With an older peer in the meeting, it stays a mesh and declines joiners above the mesh limit.
